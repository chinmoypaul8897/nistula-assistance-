01 · The idea
In six lines

A host on WhatsApp who never sleeps, never guesses, and never leaves a guest waiting.

  • One WhatsApp number. It answers the way our front desk answers — in Nistula's voice, at any hour.
  • Every price comes live from our own website. It cannot invent a number.
  • Service requests become jobs on the right staff phone — and they are chased until someone closes them.
  • A human can step in at any moment. The assistant goes quiet on that chat.
  • It remembers every guest, so a returning guest is met by someone who already knows them.
  • The rule underneath everything: never guess, never go silent.
The price you see is the final price. nistula.life · the promise the assistant is built around

Who it is for

The guest enquiring at midnight. The guest standing in an apartment who needs towels. The guest who stayed in March and is thinking about October. All three, on the same number, without anyone having to be awake.

What changes for the team

Less typing, not less control. The enquiries that arrive at odd hours are answered; the requests that arrive mid-shift turn up as a card with the house already on it. Anyone can take a chat back simply by replying in it — no button, no handover, just type.

05 · One message, in detail
The same path, every time

The assistant is bracketed by plain, predictable code on both sides. That is the whole safety idea.

  1. It arrives. We confirm it genuinely came from WhatsApp and answer "received" in under a second, so nothing is ever dropped on a slow reply.
  2. It is stored raw, first. Before any thinking. A message that arrives twice is recognised and ignored rather than answered twice.
  3. It waits about fifteen seconds. People type in bursts. Three messages become one answer, not three.
  4. The picture is assembled. Who is this, do they have a stay, are they in a villa right now, what did they tell us months ago, what does our knowledge base say.
  5. The assistant thinks, with tools. Up to five rounds. It may fetch a price, check dates, fetch the booking link, look up their booking, raise a job, remember a fact, or bring a human in.
  6. Seven checks run on the draft. Listed opposite. A failure means the reply is rewritten, replaced with approved wording, or held back and a person brought in — never sent and apologised for later.
  7. One send, through one door. Every outbound message in the whole system passes through the same gate, which knows WhatsApp's rules and the hour.

The seven checks, on every reply

  • The money check — every ₹ figure must appear in something we fetched this turn.
  • The promise check — "the team has been told" only if a job really reached a phone.
  • The negotiation check — any discount language is replaced with the approved sentence.
  • The window check — WhatsApp's rules on when a business may write first.
  • The identity check — it never claims to be a person.
  • The length and manner check — short, no exclamation marks, British English.
  • The leak check — nothing internal, and never another guest.

Why the numbered steps are grey

Steps 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 are ordinary code, not judgement. The assistant is never trusted to remember the rules — they are enforced around it, every single turn, whatever it happens to have been told.

08 · Scene two, continued · who gets nothing
The system speaks first

This is the only place the assistant writes to someone who never wrote to us. Four gates stand in front of it.

Every booking must pass all four

  • Is it recent? Bookings already in the system when we switched on get nothing. History does not suddenly start messaging people.
  • Is the stay still ahead? A guest who left in March is never told we look forward to welcoming them.
  • Is it really confirmed? An unconfirmed hold reserves nothing, so nobody is congratulated on a booking that does not exist yet.
  • Are we permitted to write to them? Direct and website bookings, yes. Airbnb and Booking.com guests, no — see opposite.

And again, at the moment of sending

Planning a message is not permission to send it. At send time it is re-checked: is this still true, has the booking been cancelled, is it the middle of the night. A stay that moves is re-planned rather than duplicated; a stay that is cancelled has its pending messages dropped.

The one everyone gets wrong

The comfortable assumption is that booking sites hide a guest's phone number, so an accidental message is impossible. Two of them do. Airbnb and Booking.com do not.

There are real people in the system right now, arriving soon, with real numbers we could dial — and nobody has told us we may. So the assistant does not, and will not, until the villa team says otherwise. That is a setting waiting on an answer, not a limitation.

Their rooms still get prepared

A booking we may not message is still a booking. It is mirrored, the stay is known, and any job it raises still reaches the right phone — because a staff card reaches no guest. The gate stops a message, not the hospitality.

09 · Scene three, continued · how it knew whose phone
Routing

Two questions, asked in order, and a fallback that is a real person every time.

First
Who does this kind of work? Towels are housekeeping. A weak AC is maintenance. "I want to speak to someone" is the front desk. The kind of request decides the role, and every kind has an answer — none of them falls through by accident.
Then
Whose round is this house on? Each person is set up with the houses they cover. The card goes to the one who covers Apartment 09.
If not
The front-desk lead. If nobody with that role covers that house — or if we could not establish the house at all — it goes to the lead, who can find out. It is never sent to whoever seems closest.
If still not
Ops. And if even ops is unset, the assistant records that nobody was reachable and does not tell the guest anyone was told — the one outcome that must never be papered over.

Why "we do not know the house" is not a small problem

It would be easy to guess — send it to whoever usually cleans the apartments. The system refuses. A guess sends a person to the wrong door and leaves a real guest waiting on a job that looks handled. Not knowing goes to a human who can find out, in seconds.

What a staff member can ask at any time

Open for you: #K7Q2FX · Apartment 09 · 2 extra towels Reply DONE <id> when finished.

One word — TASKS — and their own open list comes back. No app, no login, no training. A guest can hold at most three open requests at once, so the list stays a list.

13 · The safety rails
Checked on every reply

Eight things it is not allowed to do — enforced, not merely requested.

  • It cannot invent a price. Only the website's number reaches a guest.
  • It cannot claim work was done unless the job genuinely reached a phone.
  • It never negotiates. One rate, for everyone, always.
  • It is honest about hours. It never promises help that is not coming.
  • It says it is Nistula Assistance when asked. It never pretends to be a person.
  • It protects guest privacy. One guest can never see another's details.
  • It watches its own spending and stops itself at a set limit.
  • It brings a person in whenever it is unsure. Deferring is always allowed; guessing never is.

14 · What it never does
Five hard nos

Five things that are simply out of reach.

  • No discounts, ever. Not on request, not on repeat asking, not for anyone.
  • It never names a specific house to a guest until the team says it may.
  • It never messages guests we are not permitted to message.
  • It never keeps what a guest asks us to forget.
  • It never keeps talking once a human has taken over.
Nothing showy. Everything considered. nistula.life

Why two of these are stricter than they look

The house. A guest books an apartment, not a door number, and eZee chooses the door — sometimes late. So the assistant speaks of the apartment in Assagao, never of Apartment 09, until the team decides otherwise. The staff card is the opposite case: it must name the door, because someone has to walk to it.

Permission to write first. Guests who booked through Airbnb or Booking.com never gave us their number for WhatsApp. Until the team says we may write to them, the assistant does not — even though it holds the numbers.
